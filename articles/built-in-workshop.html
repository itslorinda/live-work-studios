Building a workshop that doesn't leave the living room

Sept 10, 2026 · 6 min read

Most ground-floor live-work units treat the "work" half as an afterthought — a corner desk, maybe a deeper closet. On a recent project we had the opposite problem: the client needed a real fabrication bench, dust collection, and material storage inside a 340 sq ft footprint that still had to read as a living room by evening.

Start with what the work actually needs

Before drawing anything, we listed the non-negotiables: a 6-foot bench with clearance on three sides, closed storage for anything sharp or loud, and a floor finish that could take a dropped chisel without showing it. Everything else — where the sofa goes, where the lighting sits — got designed around those constraints, not the other way around.

The fold-away bench did the most work here — it's the single reason the room can host a dinner two hours after someone's been sanding a table leg in it. The storage wall matters just as much for how it looks closed: from the living room side, it reads as a plain paneled wall, not a workshop door.

What we'd do differently

Dust collection is the one system you can't retrofit cheaply. If there's any chance a unit will house real fabrication work, run the ducting during framing, even if the first tenant never touches a table saw.
